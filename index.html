Black-box covert communication over LLM-agent conversations

Feedback coding embeds hidden payloads in agentic dialogue at high rate and low error — requiring no shared model, prompt, or key stream.

Abstract

AI-mediated conversations can transmit hidden information at high rate and low error, even when the two agents share no model and no prompt. Embedding signal in LLM output is not new, but doing it on ordinary, variable-length conversation — where the receiver reads only text, with no shared model, prompt, or length-scaled key stream — is where existing schemes fall short. Most inference-time methods are designed as watermarks: a signal stamped onto generated text at a fixed budget. We instead treat the token-generation process as a channel with causal, noiseless feedback and build a variable-length feedback code — Burnashev Adaptive Posterior Matching (BAM) — that carries a message reliably over whatever the conversation happens to produce. The scheme is distortion-free and provably undetectable: averaged over the secret key, the visible text carries exactly the base model's own word statistics, so no efficient test on the transcript does better than a coin flip.
